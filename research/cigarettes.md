# Cigarettes (smoking) - research dossier

Status: In research. Numbers marked **source pending** were seen only in a secondary source (web-search summary) and must be opened and checked before they go into the app. All other numbers come from abstracts read through Europe PMC (`epmc.py abs`) or from pages fetched in this session.
Levels: A = established, B = supported, C = our estimate (math shown), ? = not researched.
Design type: "assoc" = cohort/observational (a link, not proof); "causal" = randomized or experimental on humans.

| S2 | Nicotine terminal half-life **119 min (+/-44)**, computed from 90-210 min plasma samples. Matches the "about 2 h" figure. | S4 | Intravenous nicotine 0.5 mg per 70 kg raised heart rate by about **10 bpm (95% CI 7.8-12.3) at 1 min and 13 bpm (11.0-15.2) at 2 min** in 213 smokers. | S8 | A cigarette raised carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) from **3.0 +/- 1.0% to 5.2 +/- 1.7%** (mean, +2.2 points) in 6 healthy volunteers. | B (n=6) | Tashiro 1988, J Anesth, PMID 15235831 |
| S9 | Pulse CO-oximeter readings (SpCO): smokers 2.7% (SD 2.6) at baseline, 3.1% at peak during smoking; non-smoker controls 1.3%. Baseline SpCO depended on time since the last cigarette. 80% of smokers had SpCO of 5% or less. | B | Schimmel 2018, J Med Toxicol, PMID 29285649 |
| S10 | SpCO baseline 3.07 in smokers vs 1.77 in non-smokers (n=136 vs 60); SpCO and heart rate both rose during and 2 min after smoking; 83% of smokers below 5%. | B | Alatli & Kocaoglu 2025, BMC Pharmacol Toxicol, PMID 40760452, DOI 10.1186/s40360-025-00979-z |

Readings are accurate when COHb < 2.5%, over-read when COHb is high. | B | Tashiro 1988, PMID 15235831. Mechanism (dog, COHb 0-75%): Barker & Tremper 1987, Anesthesiology, PMID 3578881 |
| S13 | Chronic CO exposure can raise COHb up to 10% in heavy smokers, "functional anemia" and hypoxemia. Review statement. | B | Rahman 2025, Glob Heart, PMID 39991592 (PMC11843939) |

## 2. Long-term and habit

| ID | Claim | Level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | British doctors, 50 years (34,439 men): cigarette smokers born 1900-1930 who kept smoking died on average **about 10 years younger** than lifelong non-smokers. Quitting at 60, 50, 40, 30 gained about **3, 6, 9, 10 years**. Born 1920s: smoking tripled age-specific death rates; quitting at 50 halved the hazard, at 30 avoided almost all of it. | B (assoc, very large) | Doll 2004, BMJ, PMID 15213107 |
| L2 | US, 202,000 adults: all-cause death hazard ratio in current smokers aged 25-79 **3.0 (women, 2.7-3.3), 2.8 (men, 2.4-3.1)**; life expectancy shortened by **more than 10 years**; quitting at 25-34, 35-44, 45-54 gained about **10, 9, 6 years**; quitting before 40 cut the smoking-related death risk by about 90%. | B (assoc) | Jha 2013, N Engl J Med, PMID 23343063 |
| L3 | **Light smoking 1-4/day** (Norway, 42,722 people, follow-up to 2002): all-cause death RR **1.57 (1.33-1.85) men, 1.47 (1.19-1.82) women**; death from ischaemic heart disease RR **2.74 (2.07-3.61) men, 2.94 (1.75-4.95) women**; lung cancer RR 2.79 (0.94-8.28) men, 5.03 (1.81-13.98) women. | B (assoc) | Bjartveit & Tverdal 2005, Tob Control, PMID 16183982 |
| L4 | 141 cohort studies: coronary heart disease RR at **1 cigarette/day vs 20/day**: men **1.48 vs 2.04** (adjusted 1.74 vs 2.27); women **1.57 vs 2.84** (adjusted 2.19 vs 3.95). One cigarette/day gives **46% (men) and 31% (women)** of the excess risk of 20/day (53% and 38% adjusted). Stroke: men 1.25 vs 1.64, women 1.31 vs 2.16; excess at 1/day = 41% men, 34% women. | L5 | Lipids, 54 studies: smokers vs non-smokers - total cholesterol **+3.0%**, triglycerides **+9.1%**, VLDL-C +10.4%, LDL-C +1.7%, **HDL-C -5.7%**, apo A-I -4.2%. ## 4. Suggested bar rules (conservative, simple)

The app currently scales habits by `servings/day x how often x how long` (`habitual()` in `healthBars.ts`). Call that `h` = habitual cigarettes per day. Examples: 12 a day, every day, for years gives h=12; 12 a day, 3-5x a week, months gives 12 x 0.571 x 0.7 = 4.8.

### Sleep (today only)
- Rule: if the user smoked today and the habit is not "Just today" - **Sleep -5** (S14: +5.4 min sleep latency, and the Sleep bar already uses 1 point per extra minute). Level C.
- Smoked today, "Just today": **Sleep -2** (C, no acute data found, see note under section 1; this is a placeholder, ~ one third of the habit effect; mark "?" in the UI notes).
- Never more than -5 regardless of count (no dose-response in the abstract). Former smokers: 0 (S14).
- Note shown to user: "Nicotine half-life about 2 h (S2): 4 h after a cigarette a quarter is still in the blood (0.5^2 = 0.25)." Level C math.
- Caffeine interaction: for the espresso model, smoker half-life = 6.0 h x 0.58 = 3.5 h (S19), so evening coffee clears faster; apply only if user is a daily smoker.

### Cholesterol (habit)
- Craig (L5): total cholesterol +1.8% (light), +4.3% (moderate), +4.5% (heavy). Existing cola rule uses 1.1 point per 1% (cola +3.6% = -4). Same scale gives about -2, -4.7, -5.
- Rule: **Cholesterol -0.4 per habitual cigarette/day (-0.4 x h), capped at -5** (reached at h=12.5). Level C. Examples: h=3 gives -1.2; h=8 gives -3.2; h=15 gives -5.
- Cap is deliberately close to Craig's 4.5% x 1.1. Triglycerides and HDL (-5.7%, -4.6 to -8.9%) are not separate bars; keep them in the note text only.
- Quit effect: +2.4 mg/dL HDL after 1 y (L6) - note only, no bar change, since LDL did not change.

### Life quality (habit)
- Anchor: about 10 years lost (L1, L2). Scale used in the app is arbitrary, so we map **1 life-year = 1 point**, capped at -10. Level C.
- Smoking is not linear: 1 cigarette/day carries about half the CVD excess of 20/day (L4), and 1-4/day already has all-cause RR 1.5 (L3). So use steps on `h`:

| h (habitual cigarettes/day) | Life quality |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 0 < h <= 5 | -4 (about 40% of 10; L4 says 31-53% for 1/day) |
| 5 < h <= 10 | -6 |
| 10 < h <= 20 | -8 |
| h > 20 | -10 |

- Steps in the middle are interpolation (C), not measured. Do NOT multiply by the cola/coffee precedent (-3 per +9% risk): applying it to RR 1.5 would give -17 or worse and swamp the bar.
- Possible former-smoker rule (needs a new input, see Open questions): remaining penalty = -10 x (1 - saved years / 10) where saved years = 3, 6, 9, 10 for quitting at 60, 50, 40, 30 (L1).

### Fat
- No bonus for being thinner (L8: smokers 2.7 kg lighter, but that is not health). Current smoker: **no change** to Fat.
- Quitter: weight gain 4.6 kg in year 1 (L9). For a 70 kg, 1.75 m user BMI rises about 4.6 / 1.75^2 = 1.5. Optional note only.

### Sugar
- No effect on daily sugar. Diabetes link (L7) is captured in Life quality; do not also subtract on Sugar (double counting).

### Health
- Unchanged: average of the other five bars.

### Notes to show in the "How is this calculated?" panel
- "Cigarettes: 10 years of life lost on average (Doll 2004; Jha 2013). One cigarette a day carries about half the heart risk of twenty (Hackshaw 2018). A link, not proof."

## 5. Suggested acute model (per cigarette)

Each cigarette adds an effect that rises fast and then decays. Effects of several cigarettes add (superposition) except that heart rate and blood pressure saturate (S5: later cigarettes reached the same peak as the first, so cap the sum at the single-cigarette peak plus 20%). Level C for all time constants; amplitudes from sources noted.

| Signal | Amplitude per cigarette | Rise | Decay | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | +10 bpm to +15 bpm (central +12) | peak at ~5 min (S1; IV nicotine reaches +10 at 1 min and +13 at 2 min, S4) | exponential, time constant 20 min, so ~5% left at 60 min | S4 measured; S7 "10-20 bpm up to 60 min" **source pending** |
| Systolic BP | +7 mmHg (range 5-10) | peak at ~5 min | time constant 10 min, ~5% left at 30 min | S3 direction measured; S7 numbers **source pending** |
| Diastolic BP | about 60% of systolic rise (C, guess; no source read) | same | same | **?** |
| COHb | +2.2 points (S8) | immediate (minutes) | half-life 320 min, i.e. time constant 320 / ln2 = 462 min; range 128-409 min | S8 measured (n=6); half-life **source pending** (S11) |
| Nicotine in blood | relative units: 1 at peak | peak at 6 min (S1: 5-8) | half-life 119 min (S2), k = ln2/119 = 0.0058 per min | S1, S2 |

Formulas, with t in minutes since the cigarette:
- HR(t) = HR0 + 12 x (1 - exp(-t/2.5)) x exp(-t/20) (the 2.5 min rise time puts the peak at about 5-6 min).
- SBP(t) = SBP0 + 7 x (1 - exp(-t/2.5)) x exp(-t/10).
- COHb(t) = COHb0 + 2.2 x exp(-t/462) per cigarette, summed over all cigarettes of the day. Baseline COHb0: 1.3% for non-smokers, 2.7-3.1% for smokers (S9, S10).
- Nicotine(t) = exp(-0.0058 x t), summed.

Worked examples (level C math):
- One cigarette, 4 h later (240 min): COHb adds 2.2 x exp(-240/462) = 2.2 x 0.595 = 1.3 points; nicotine left = 0.5^(240/119) = 0.25.
- 10 cigarettes spread over 16 h (about one per 96 min): each leaves 2.2 x exp(-96/462) = 1.8 after one interval; sum of the geometric series 2.2 / (1 - 0.81) = 11.6 points - far above measured steady daily levels (S9, S10: 3 to 4%). So the single-cigarette +2.2 (n=6, S8) probably over-states the rise in regular smokers, or the half-life is shorter. **Do not ship the stacking COHb curve without checking S8/S11**; fallback: cap COHb at 10% (S13) and use a baseline of 3% from S9/S10 for daily smokers.
- SpO2 display: reading error = 1.06 x COHb - 2.49 (S12). At COHb 3% error +0.7 points; at 5.2% error +3.0; at 10% about +8. So a smoker's pulse oximeter shows normal or higher SpO2 while the oxygen actually carried by blood is lower by about the COHb value. The app should show "SpO2 reading looks normal; true oxygen-carrying capacity down about COHb %".
- Check for the app's vitals (daily.ts/vitals-circadian.md): HR and BP curves are added to the existing circadian curves; no change to sleep anchors except Sleep rule above.
