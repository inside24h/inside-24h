# GI additions for foods missing a GI in fooddb.json

All values: glycaemic index, glucose = 100, mean +/- SEM where printed. Nothing here is estimated.

## Sources actually read

- **Atkinson FS, Foster-Powell K, Brand-Miller JC. International Tables of Glycemic Index and Glycemic Load Values: 2008. Diabetes Care 31(12):2281-2283. PMC2584181.**
  - **Table 1** (main text, 62 foods): "average GI of 62 common foods derived from multiple studies by different laboratories". It does not give the number of studies per food.
  - **Supplementary Table A1**: GI/GL values in subjects with **normal glucose tolerance** (1,879 entries). Each row is one published or unpublished test. SEM is printed on the directly measured value.
  - **Supplementary Table A2**: values from subjects with **impaired glucose tolerance or diabetes, small subject numbers, or wide variability**. Rows marked "Normal, 5" are healthy but have only 5 subjects.
  - Retrieved from Europe PMC: `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC2584181/supplementaryFiles`. The files are `dc08-1239_1.pdf` (A1) and `dc08-1239_2.pdf` (A2).
- **Foster-Powell 2002 AJCN (PMID 12081815): not read.** It is not open access in Europe PMC, so nothing below comes from it.
- Where the table prints two GI columns, the first is glucose = 100 (used here). The second is bread = 100, so ignore it.
- A1 and A2 rows were extracted from the PDFs by text layout. Where a row's name and numbers sat on adjacent lines, I checked the line positions (beer, kiwi, mango, nectarine) and the pairing is consistent. Rows quoted without SEM carry none in the table (usually a bread-reference test converted to glucose scale).
- "Healthy" means the Subjects column says "Normal". Numbers after it are the subject count. "Ref." is the table's reference number, which I did not look up.

## Beer caveat

- The paper reports one beer test: **Toohey's New, GI 66 +/- 7**, 9 normal subjects. Its footnote 7 says test and reference portions each contained 10 g carbohydrate. The serve in that row is 250 mL with 8 g available carbohydrate, so GL is about 5.
- The GI is defined per 10 g carbohydrate, so the alcohol itself is not part of the figure. The glycaemic effect of beer is small because the carbohydrate is small. The value says nothing about alcohol or ethanol metabolism.
- It is a single-product, single-study value and not a multi-lab mean.
- A search of Europe PMC for beer GI, lager, maltose and glucose response returned no further GI measurements. I did not find or use any maltose-based surrogate.
- A published maltose GI value would be a different thing (maltose is not what is measured in the beer test), so I am not substituting one.

## Foods with no usable value in these sources

- **Sweetened iced tea**: not found.
- **Energy drink (Red Bull type)**: not found. Sports-drink rows exist (listed above). Keep Table 1 "soft drink/soda" 59 +/- 3 as the nearest class, flagged as a class value.
- **Burek / filo-cheese pastry**: not found. Puff pastry 56 +/- 5 is a different food.
- **Croissant, plum, fresh peach, fresh pear**: measured only in people with diabetes or IGT (Table A2), so there is no healthy-subject value.
- **Margherita / plain cheese pizza in healthy subjects**: not found. Only different Pizza Hut and Loblaw pizzas.
- **Fresh mandarin**: only canned in juice.

## Counts

- Verified with a direct healthy-subject entry: 26 foods (beer, doughnut, sponge cake [5 subjects, weakest], biscuits, pancakes, crackers, chocolate milk, hamburger, baguette, rye bread, grapes, cherries, kiwi, mango, strawberries, cantaloupe, carrots, peas, white/haricot beans, baked beans, hummus, mashed potato, chips/crisps, ice cream, plain yogurt, smoothie).
- Proxy only: pizza, mandarin (canned), burek (puff pastry).
- Diabetic-only: croissant, plum, fresh peach, fresh pear.
- Not found: iced tea, energy drink.
- Secondary (non-paper) sources: none used.
