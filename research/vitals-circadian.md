# Vitals over 24 h: natural variation + caffeine (HR, BP, glucose, SpO2)

Scope: healthy, non-diabetic adult at rest (seated/supine), sea level. Purpose: replace flat lines in "Inside 24h" with evidence-based curves for "you" vs "healthy person of same size", e.g. espresso at 16:00, bedtime 23:00.

Evidence rule: every number below was read in the abstract or full text of the cited source during this research session (Europe PMC helper; two items via web pages, marked). Level: A = consistent human evidence / guideline, B = small or variable human studies, C = derived estimate (our calculation or modelling choice).

## Suggested model

Time t in clock hours. Define `s(t)` = sleep fraction (0 awake, 1 asleep), built from bedtime `T_bed`, sleep onset latency `SOL`, wake time `T_wake`:
- `s` ramps 0→1 over ~20 min starting at `T_bed + SOL` (C; H7 shows stepwise falls, H8 shows slower fall with long SOL).
- `s` ramps 1→0 over ~15 min at `T_wake` (C).
- Caffeine: `SOL += ΔSOL`, `T_wake` unchanged so TST shrinks; optional WASO bouts set `s = 0` for a few minutes (S1, S2).

`cosC(t, peak) = cos(2π (t − peak) / 24)`

**Heart rate (resting)**
`HR(t) = HR_rest + A_HR · cosC(t, 16.6) − D_HR · s(t) + ΔHR_caf(t)`
- `HR_rest` (resting awake mean): user input; healthy default 65–75 bpm (H1, H5).
- `A_HR` = 4 bpm, peak 16:36, trough ~04:36 (H4, H5; trough time C).
- `D_HR` = 6 bpm (range 4–8) (H9, H6). With A_HR, gives total resting-day to deep-night drop ~12–14 bpm; ambulatory comparison ~15 bpm (H2).
- `ΔHR_caf` awake: 0 (range −3 to +2) (C11). Asleep, caffeine in body: 0 default, uncertainty band ±3 bpm (C7 vs C8). Any caffeine-induced WASO shows up as awake periods (HR jumps to awake level).

**Blood pressure**
`SBP(t) = SBP_day · (1 − dip · s(t)) + 2 · cosC(t, 13.7) + ΔSBP_caf(t)`; same form for DBP.
- `SBP_day`/`DBP_day`: user; healthy reference < 120/70 (B5).
- `dip` = 0.13 (range 0.10–0.20) (B1, B2).
- Circadian term semi-amplitude 2 mmHg (peak-to-trough 4, B3), trough 01:40 → peak 13:40 (B4; peak by symmetry, C).
- Morning surge: produced by the `s` ramp; let the post-wake rise reach day level over ~1–2 h (ramp `s` 1→0 for BP over 60–90 min instead of 15 min) → surge 12–24 mmHg (B6, B8).
- `ΔSBP_caf(t) = E · dose-scaled · f(t)`, E = +4 mmHg SBP / +3 DBP at ~100 mg, up to +8/+6 at 200–300 mg; f(t) rises to 1 by 60 min, holds to ~3 h, decays (half ~2 h) (C9, C8, C2; shape C). Halve for habitual users (C10).

**Glucose (fasting/between meals; meals handled elsewhere)**
`G(t) = G_base − 3 · s(t)` mg/dL, plus slow noise (±3 mg/dL/h)
- `G_base` = 93 mg/dL (5.2 mmol/L) premeal (G3); 24-h mean with meals ~95–102 (G1–G3).
- Overnight: flat after the post-dinner decline; overnight mean ~97 vs day 105 includes meals (G2, G4). The −3 term is a C choice.
- Dawn phenomenon in healthy adults: 0 (G4). Caffeine/coffee: 0 (G6).

**SpO2**
`SpO2(t) = S0 + 0.4 · cosC(t, 12)` (%), with `S0` = 95.8 (age < 60) or ~95.1 for > 60 (O1, O3)
- Gives ~96.2 day / ~95.4 night (O1). Sleep dips to ~94.7 occur 10% of the night; occasional brief lows ~90% are normal (O3) — optional rendering.
- Caffeine: 0 (O4).

**"You" vs "healthy person of same size"**: same equations; "you" uses user inputs (HR_rest, SBP/DBP_day, age) and caffeine terms; healthy reference uses defaults above with no caffeine.

## Unknowns

- No meta-analysis of caffeine on resting HR; direction is inconsistent (decrease in C4, C6, C7; increase at 360 mg C3; higher overnight HR C8). Default 0 with uncertainty band.
- Exact time constants of HR/BP fall at sleep onset and rise after waking (minutes) not quantified in sources read; ramp lengths are C choices.
- Split of the night HR dip into sleep vs circadian parts is only qualitative (H6); D_HR is derived (H9).
- Typical (median) morning BP surge in healthy normotensives not found as a number; only risk-decile cutoffs (B7).
- Drake 2013 6-h TST effect: text (1.1–1.2 h) and table (−44 min) disagree (S3).
- Caffeine dose-response for sleep between 100 and 400 mg is not directly measured; interpolation is C. Meta-analysis (S1) did not give a per-mg slope in the abstract.
- Caffeine pharmacokinetics (half-life) not covered here; see espresso research. Saliva caffeine was still elevated before bed after afternoon doses in S2 (values not extracted).
- Glucose overnight drop size (−3 mg/dL) and SpO2 cosine fit are derived from group means; individual variability is large (SD ~1.3–1.5% SpO2; HR amplitude SD 1.9 bpm).
- Most caffeine-sleep RCTs are in young men; effects in women, older adults, slow CYP1A2 metabolisers not established here.
