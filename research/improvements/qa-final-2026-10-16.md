## Open: needs a decision (not changed)
1. **Cola kcal differ**: search and the database say 144 kcal per can, while the curated story, Quick answers and Compare say 139. (Science/data, Opus decision.)
2. **"Glass" = 250 ml for every drink**: typing "a glass of wine" still gives 250 ml / 211 kcal. A wine glass is usually 125–175 ml. (Parser data.)
3. **Espresso and sleep**: 63 mg at 20:45 leaves about 51 mg at 23:00, yet the app says "too little to matter" and "any time today". Check the threshold against Drake 2013 / EFSA. (Science.)
4. **Walking minutes rise with extreme profiles**: at 200 kg, 130 cm and 100 years, 139 kcal = 1 h 5 min, against 32 min by default. Heavier people should burn it faster. Check the age/height terms. (Science.)
5. **Every-day panel**: "Type 2 diabetes +13–18% · A · Link, not proof". Pairing level A with "link, not proof" confuses readers. "Risk of dying early ≈ +9% (C)" is alarming for a C-level estimate. Consider softer wording or hiding it in Simple. (Science/copy.)
6. **Compare and video still use mmol/L only** (chart, cards, video overlay), even when the user picked mg/dL.
7. **Pro "Your history: everything you ate this week"** contradicts upute §0 ("we don't do a food diary"). Product decision.
8. **Support** offers only a raw Solana address ("PayPal: coming soon"). A journalist may read crypto-only donations as a red flag. Add a mainstream option before press.
9. The beer answer "Will my blood sugar rise? Hard to say…" has a green (fine) edge. A neutral grey would be more honest.
10. Detailed view: the evidence badges are in "A C B" order, and the disclaimer at the bottom of the left column is cut off at 1440x900 (you can scroll to it).
11. The 3rd Tab stop of the native time input has no visible focus ring (Chrome's segment), and the search dropdown adds about 25 Tab stops (presets plus "Add X") before the header.
12. Video export to the end and the iOS Safari share sheet were not verified (software GL here). Test on a real iPhone before launch.

## Verdict
After these fixes the product is close to launch quality: a clear first screen, consistent numbers across label, chapter, answers and share card, honest ranges, solid keyboard and screen-reader basics, and no crashes. The remaining gaps are mostly data and science consistency (items 1–5) and two trust items for press (7–8). None of them is a visible bug, but a careful journalist could find each one.
